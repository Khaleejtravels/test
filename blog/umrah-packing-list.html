What to Pack for Umrah: A Practical Packing List

5 min read · Khaleej Travels

Packing for Umrah is different from packing for an ordinary trip — you're dressing for ihram, long hours on your feet, and mosque etiquette, not just a holiday. Here's a practical list based on what people actually end up needing.

Documents (carry-on, not checked luggage)

  • Passport with your visa, plus 2-3 photocopies kept separately from the original
  • Printed flight and hotel booking confirmations
  • A small pouch or folder to keep everything together and easy to grab at checkpoints

For ihram (men)

  • At least two sets of ihram sheets — one to wear, one as backup
  • An ihram belt or pouch to hold money and small items, since ihram cloth has no pockets
  • Unscented soap and non-perfumed toiletries (perfume is avoided once in ihram)

For ihram (women)

  • Loose, modest, non-form-fitting clothing in any color — there's no fixed color requirement
  • A simple headscarf that stays in place easily during long hours of walking

Footwear

  • Comfortable sandals that slip on and off easily — you'll be removing and re-wearing shoes constantly at mosque entrances
  • A lightweight bag to carry your shoes inside the mosque, since shelves fill up fast during busy hours

Everyday essentials

  • A refillable water bottle — Zamzam water is available everywhere, but having your own bottle helps during long queues
  • A small prayer mat or mat cover, useful for outdoor prayer areas during peak crowds
  • A power bank and a universal adapter (Saudi Arabia uses UK-style three-pin sockets)
  • Basic medication — pain relief, rehydration salts, and anything you personally take regularly
  • Sunscreen and lip balm, especially if travelling in the summer months

What people always forget

  • A physical copy of emergency contacts — phones die or get lost in crowds, and having a card with your hotel address and family contact number is worth the two minutes it takes to write.
  • Small denomination Saudi riyals for tipping, small purchases, or when card machines are down.
  • A lightweight jacket — mosque interiors and hotel air conditioning can be surprisingly cold even in hot weather.

What to leave at home

Skip heavy luggage, valuable jewelry, and anything you'd be devastated to lose in a crowd. Umrah involves a lot of walking, queuing, and shared spaces — travel as light as you comfortably can.
